# AstroFlame AI
### Exploring Microgravity Combustion for Safer Spaceflight

Developed for the **NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — Flame in Freefall**.

AstroFlame AI is an interactive research exploration platform that makes NASA's microgravity combustion research easier to understand and investigate. It uses data from NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI-26) Burning and Suppression of Solids (BASS) investigation.

## Project Overview

Fire behaves differently in microgravity because buoyancy-driven airflow is greatly reduced. This changes how heat, oxygen, and combustion products move around flames, making combustion research important for future spaceflight safety.

AstroFlame AI organizes NASA's research datasets into an interactive dashboard, allowing users to explore experimental records, planned configurations, and scientific background in one place.

## Features

### 1. ISS BASS Combustion Explorer
- Filter experimental records by fuel-material category and sample geometry.
- Explore dataset summaries and visualizations.
- Examine the available experimental records.

### 2. BASS Configuration Explorer
- Explore planned experimental configurations.
- Investigate fuel samples, sample identifiers, and planned test points.
- Examine how different experimental setups are organized.

### 3. Scientific Methodology
- Understand the scientific context of combustion in microgravity.
- Explore the NASA data sources used.
- Learn about dataset limitations and responsible interpretation.

## Scientific Accuracy and Data Limitations

AstroFlame AI is designed as a research exploration platform, not a predictive fire-safety system.

The project distinguishes between:
- **Experimental records:** Entries in the NASA BASS dataset.
- **Planned configurations:** Experimental setups described in the planned test matrix.

Planned configurations should not be interpreted as completed experiments or measured combustion outcomes. The available datasets do not, by themselves, establish material safety rankings, fire-risk scores, or validated predictions of flame behavior.

The platform aims to make NASA research more accessible while avoiding conclusions that the available data cannot support.
